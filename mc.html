Region Overview

11 Modules.
Both Directions.

The motor cortex is uniquely informative because it shows bidirectional changes in ALS — not just a uniform loss of gene expression. Six modules are significantly downregulated and five are significantly upregulated. This contrast reflects two simultaneous biological processes: the death and transcriptional silencing of motor neurons (DOWN modules), and the activation of reactive microglial and astrocytic programmes in response to neuronal damage (UP modules).

The DOWN modules contain exceptionally clear neuronal gene content: acetylcholinesterase (ACHE), nicotinic and muscarinic acetylcholine receptors (CHRNB2, CHRM4), vesicular acetylcholine transporter (SLC18A3), endocannabinoid synthesis enzymes (DAGLA), and hereditary spastic paraplegia genes (REEP2). This is a direct transcriptional readout of cholinergic synaptic programme loss in ALS.

Downregulated Modules — Neuronal Loss

What ALS Takes Away

These six modules represent gene programmes being lost as ALS motor neurons degenerate. The biological functions encoded are strikingly neuronal — cholinergic synaptic transmission, endocannabinoid signalling, ER morphology maintenance.

The largest and most significant downregulated module, packed with neuronal biology. REEP2 mutations cause hereditary spastic paraplegia (SPG72) — its appearance in the top ALS module suggests shared ER-morphology pathways between ALS and HSP. IQSEC3 regulates GABAergic synapse function. ADGRB1 (BAI1) drives synaptogenesis. DAGLA synthesises 2-arachidonoylglycerol, the major endocannabinoid at excitatory synapses. Together, these genes define a broad synaptic and neuronal architecture programme lost in ALS motor cortex.

A compelling cholinergic neurotransmission module. ACHE (acetylcholinesterase) is the enzyme that terminates cholinergic neurotransmission at synapses — its downregulation marks cholinergic neuron loss. CHRNB2 (nicotinic ACh receptor β2) and CHRM4 (muscarinic ACh receptor M4) are key receptor subunits. The coordinated downregulation of this entire cholinergic gene set is a strong transcriptional signature of motor neuron loss in ALS.

SLC18A3 encodes the vesicular acetylcholine transporter (VAChT) — the protein that loads acetylcholine into synaptic vesicles in cholinergic neurons. It is one of the most specific markers of cholinergic identity. Finding SLC18A3 in a downregulated module confirms that cholinergic neurons and their synaptic machinery are specifically lost in ALS motor cortex. SOX14 and HMX2 are transcription factors involved in interneuron and motor neuron development.

PEX14 is essential for peroxisome biogenesis — peroxisomal dysfunction has been linked to ALS pathology through impaired fatty acid oxidation and oxidative stress. ATP6V0E2 (vacuolar ATPase) maintains lysosomal acidification, critical for protein degradation. Loss of these housekeeping cellular maintenance genes may reflect ALS motor neurons losing metabolic homeostasis capacity before death.

Upregulated Modules — Reactive Response

What ALS Switches On

Five modules increase in ALS — not because neurons are gaining function, but because reactive glial and stress-response gene programmes are ramping up in response to neuronal damage.

EXOSC8FANCLCNOT2 TSEN15MAP4K5ANKRD10

EXOSC8 is a component of the RNA exosome complex, which degrades aberrant and excess RNA. FANCL is the E3 ubiquitin ligase of the Fanconi anaemia DNA repair pathway. CNOT2 is part of the CCR4-NOT deadenylase complex regulating mRNA stability. Upregulation of RNA quality control and DNA damage repair machinery suggests that surviving cells in ALS motor cortex are responding to genotoxic and RNA-processing stress — consistent with TDP-43 pathology disrupting RNA homeostasis.

Biological Interpretation

Two Stories, One Tissue

The motor cortex data tells two simultaneous stories, which can be clearly separated by examining the direction of each module's ALS correlation.

↓ The Death of Motor Neurons

The DOWN modules (greenyellow, cyan, darkslateblue) contain a remarkably coherent set of cholinergic and synaptic genes: ACHE, CHRNB2, CHRM4, SLC18A3, DAGLA, REEP2, IQSEC3, ADGRB1. These are the genes that define the identity and function of motor and projection neurons. Their coordinated loss in ALS is the transcriptional signature of upper motor neuron degeneration — the defining pathology of ALS motor cortex. This goes beyond cell death: it captures the progressive silencing of neuronal identity before cells are fully lost.

↑ The Response of Surviving Cells

The UP modules (brown4, lightcyan1) contain RNA quality control and DNA repair genes: EXOSC8, FANCL, CNOT2, TSEN15, CCNH. These are stress-response programmes activated as surviving cells cope with the molecular consequences of ALS pathology — protein aggregation (TDP-43, FUS), RNA processing disruption, and genotoxic stress. The upregulation of RNA exosome components (EXOSC8) is particularly relevant given TDP-43's central role in RNA metabolism.

Key insight: The bidirectional nature of motor cortex ALS modules means that studying gene expression changes in ALS motor cortex using only bulk RNA-seq and looking at "up vs. down" regulated genes in a simple differential expression framework would produce a confusingly mixed picture. WGCNA's co-expression framework — and the RIN independence filter — cleanly separates these two biological programmes into distinct modules, allowing their independent interpretation and biological meaning to emerge.
